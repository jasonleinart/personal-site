**Purpose:** Reference document for evolving site positioning, service offerings, and content strategy
**Status:** Living document — revisit as advisory/training model takes shape

## Context

Sundeep Teki runs a premium AI career coaching and advisory practice. His site is worth studying not as a direct competitor (different market, different positioning) but as a reference for how someone packages AI expertise into coaching, consulting, and training offerings.

**Key difference in positioning:**
- Sundeep's credentials and outcomes speak for themselves (Oxford/UCL PhD, Amazon Alexa AI, 100+ FAANG placements). He doesn't need to "show the work" — the brand names do that.
- My site is designed to demonstrate capability to hiring managers as I transition from marketing into IT/digital transformation/AI work. The case studies, playbook, and decision records *are* the proof because the credential stack isn't there yet.

These are fundamentally different strategies for building authority. His depreciates if AI democratizes credentials. Mine appreciates as the body of published work grows.

## Where Sundeep Is Strong

### Credential stacking
Oxford/UCL PhD → Amazon Alexa AI → startup leadership. Each element reinforces the next. The FAANG logo bar (Google, Apple, Meta, Amazon, LinkedIn, Salesforce) says "my clients land here" without explanation.

### Role-specific targeting
Four AI roles (Forward Deployed Engineer, Research Engineer, Research Scientist, AI Engineer) with specific salary outcomes ($350K+ TC). Visitors immediately know if they're in the right place.

### Transparent coaching pricing
$349 → $999 → $3,999 creates a clear ladder. Visitors self-qualify before the free call.

### Quantified social proof
100+ FAANG placements, 20+ countries, 50-100% salary increases. Specific enough to feel real.

### Multiple consulting engagement models
Monthly subscription, project-based (3mo minimum), fractional Head of AI, one-off. Each maps to different buyer maturity.

### Three-tier training structure
Covers the full organizational stack: executive strategy, technical deep-dive, strategic workshop.

## Where Sundeep Is Weak (Gap Opportunities)

### Short-Term (0-3 months) — Things I could act on now

**No technical proof-of-work.** His site is Weebly. No demos, tools, or working prototypes. Everything is "trust my credentials." I already have the contract review AI tool, the Astro/Cloudflare stack, and documented case studies. This is a real differentiator for the buyer who wants to see capability, not just hear about it.

**No methodology documentation.** He describes a 3-phase coaching method but doesn't publish reusable frameworks. My 6-phase playbook is published, detailed, and works as both a sales tool and a content asset.

**No email capture or lead magnets.** No newsletter signup, no downloadable resources. It's "book a call" or nothing. Even a simple assessment or checklist would create a mid-funnel touchpoint.

**No decision documentation.** He never shows how he thinks. Clients take it on faith. My decision records and analysis pieces show reasoning process — which is what organizations actually evaluate in a consultant or hire.

### Medium-Term (3-6 months) — Structural advantages to build

**No self-service products.** Nothing between the free 15-min call and $3,999 coaching. No courses, templates, or async offerings. A productized offering (AI readiness assessment template, recorded workshop, team capability audit) could capture the "not ready for 1:1 but want to start" audience.

**No organizational buyer path.** His consulting page reads like expanded coaching. No frameworks for how an organization should approach AI adoption. My Three Horizons framework (personal-ai, team-ai, organizational-ai) already maps the buyer journey at every level.

**Narrow target market.** Locked into FAANG-aspiring individual engineers. Real market, but it's career coaching — not transformation work. The organizational buyer (VP of Ops at a 200-person company wondering how to implement AI) is a different buyer with larger contracts and stickier relationships.

**No content engine.** Blog exists but cadence is unclear. No visible newsletter. Content doesn't compound. Regular analysis pieces plus notes plus a newsletter creates authority that builds on itself.

### Long-Term (6-12 months) — Market position to claim

**Doesn't scale past 1:1.** Every dollar requires his time. No group programs, community, or platform play.

**No mid-market positioning.** He serves individuals and enterprises. The $2M-$50M company with no AI team and no FAANG budget is invisible to him.

**No "show your work" culture.** Authority comes from credentials and past employers. That's a depreciating asset. Authority from published thinking, working prototypes, and transparent methodology appreciates over time.

**No writing depth.** Papers are academic (neuroscience). Blog is sparse. No signature frameworks that get referenced by others. Analysis pieces (agents vs automation, AI-native work, change management) are the kind of content that builds organic discovery.

## What This Means for My Site Evolution

### I am NOT a coach
Sundeep's coaching model works because he's placing people at specific companies for specific salaries. That's a clear transaction. My path is different — I'm building toward training, workshops, speaking, and potentially advisory work around AI transformation methodology.

### Training & workshops are the near-term opportunity
Sundeep's three-tier training structure (executive, technical, strategic) is a good reference model. I could develop something similar mapped to my playbook phases:
- Workshop: "AI Readiness Assessment" (maps to Phase 1-2 of playbook)
- Workshop: "AI Governance for Mid-Market" (maps to Phase 4)
- Training: "AI-Native Project Management" (ties to PMI speaking topic)

### Speaking is the gateway
The PMI Great Lakes Spring Symposium 2026 talk on AI-native project management is the right entry point. A strong speaking engagement leads to workshop inquiries, which leads to organizational relationships. Get the speaking page right first.

### Lead magnets from the playbook — maybe
The playbook tools and templates could work as lead magnets, but only if:
- They're genuinely useful standalone (not just teasers)
- They create a natural path to "want help implementing this? Let's talk"
- They don't give away so much that the advisory conversation becomes unnecessary

A single assessment tool (AI readiness scorecard, team capability checklist) is probably better than packaging full playbook phases as downloads.

## Subdomain Decision: ai.jasonleinart.com

**Decision: Don't do it.**

Reasons:
- Google treats subdomains as separate sites for SEO. Splits domain authority instead of concentrating it.
- The entire site is AI systems and strategy. A subdomain implies AI is a side offering.
- Creates maintenance overhead (two deployments, two designs, link confusion).
- Visitors on the subdomain might never discover the playbook, projects, or analysis.

**Instead:** Use path-based organization on the main domain. `/advisory`, `/training`, `/workshops` as the service model takes shape. The Three Horizons section already provides a smart organizational scheme.

**Exception:** If a standalone SaaS product or tool emerges (AI readiness assessment platform, for example), that could warrant its own domain. But services stay unified.
